### Q2. Citation numbers

A citation number in Gemini Notebook is a reference that connects a particular part of the notebook's answer to the source from which the information was taken.

Whenever I see a citation number, I should click it, open the original source, and read the exact line it refers to. This helps me check whether the source genuinely supports the information provided by the notebook instead of trusting the AI answer without verification.

### Q3. Context-test questions

Before writing the prompt in Step 2, I had to consider these three context-test questions:

1. **Who is the audience?** Rohan is my friend, and he has not watched *Kantara*. Therefore, the explanation should be understandable to someone who is new to the film.

2. **What does the audience need to decide?** Rohan needs to decide whether he should watch *Kantara* this weekend. So, the explanation should give him interesting reasons to consider watching it.

3. **What format feels right?** A short, casual message written like a WhatsApp conversation would be more suitable than a formal essay or a long Wikipedia-style explanation.

### Q5. Prompt change

The section of my prompt that made the biggest difference was:

> "My friend Rohan hasn't seen Kantara and wants to know if he should watch it this weekend. Write the explanation in a casual, friendly tone, without revealing the ending."

This made a difference because it clearly identified the audience, their situation, and the purpose of the explanation. Instead of giving general information about the film, the AI focused on giving Rohan interesting reasons to watch it while avoiding spoilers.

# HARD

### Q7. Comparing outputs

The output from the simple prompt, "Tell me about Kantara", was more general and focused on giving basic information about the film. It was not specifically written for Rohan or his situation.

However, the context-engineered explanation was more personal, shorter, and focused on three interesting reasons why Rohan might enjoy watching the film. It also avoided ending spoilers and used a casual tone, as requested. Therefore, the second explanation was more useful because it considered the actual reader, their purpose, and the format they needed.

### Q8. Next time

If I had to complete this sprint for another film, I would make two improvements:

1. **Notebook setup:** I would choose sources from different reliable publications and check their relevance before importing them. I would also make sure that the sources cover different aspects of the film, such as its storyline, cultural background, reviews, and audience response. This would give me better information and reduce the chances of depending on only one source.

2. **Prompt writing:** I would make my prompt more specific by clearly mentioning the audience, purpose, important points to include, information to avoid, and the expected format. I would also mention that every factual claim must be based on my notebook sources. This would help the AI produce a more focused, relevant, and properly supported explanation.

**Conclusion**
Through this sprint, I learned that getting a useful AI answer is not just about asking a question. It is also about collecting relevant sources, providing the right context, and checking the information before using it. I understood that AI-generated answers should not be accepted blindly, even when they include citations. By combining source verification with context engineering, I can create explanations that are more accurate, useful, and suitable for a specific audience.
